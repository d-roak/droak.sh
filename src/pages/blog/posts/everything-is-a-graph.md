## thesis

every system — physical, biological, social, computational — is fundamentally a graph. nodes and edges aren't a metaphor; they're the underlying structure.

---

## structure

### 1. what is a graph (briefly)

- nodes: entities
- edges: relationships
- directed vs undirected, weighted vs unweighted
- not the chart kind — the mathematical kind

### 2. graphs in nature

- neural networks in the brain (literally)
- protein interaction networks
- food webs and ecosystems
- river deltas, vascular systems, mycelium

### 3. graphs in society

- social networks (obvious) but also trust networks, power structures
- supply chains
- city road networks and how they self-organize
- language: words as nodes, co-occurrence as edges

### 4. graphs in computation

- the internet is a graph
- git history is a DAG
- dependency trees, call graphs, state machines
- compilers: ASTs, CFGs, SSA form
- databases: foreign key relationships, knowledge graphs

### 5. graphs in physics

- spacetime as a network (loop quantum gravity)
- feynman diagrams are literally graphs
- phase transitions modeled as percolation on graphs

### 6. the real claim

- not just that graphs are a useful model — that the universe is relational at its core
- things don't have intrinsic properties; they have relational properties
- matter, meaning, causality — all emerge from structure

### 7. implications

- understanding a system = understanding its graph structure
- changing a system = rewiring edges
- "debugging" the world: find the wrong edges
